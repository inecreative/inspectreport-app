Switching Home Inspection Report Software: A Migration Checklist That Loses Nothing

November 14, 2026 · InspectReport AI

Most inspectors who hate their report software keep paying for it anyway. Not because the software is good — because switching feels risky. Your templates live in the old tool. Years of report history live there. Your comment library, your pricing, your client list. One wrong move and a Tuesday inspection turns into a scramble.

The fear is reasonable. The risk is smaller than it looks. A software switch is a weekend job, not a project, if you do it in the right order: export first, rebuild second, run both in parallel, and cancel last. Here is the checklist, in the order that loses nothing.

Step 1: Export everything before you touch anything

Do this while your old account is still fully active. Once a subscription lapses, export tools sometimes lapse with it — some vendors retire export access on a deadline, so never assume you can come back later.

  • Your templates and comment library. Export every template you actually use, including the ones you only use twice a year. Screenshots work if there is no export button; a template you can read is a template you can rebuild.
  • Your past reports as PDFs. Download the reports you may need again — the last year at minimum, and any report tied to a warranty, a dispute, or an ongoing transaction. A PDF in your own files is yours forever; a report inside a cancelled account is not.
  • Your client and agent contacts. Export the list with names, emails, and phone numbers. This is the piece people forget, and it is the piece that hurts most to rebuild from memory.
  • Your price book, if you have one. Repair-cost line items and the prices you charge for them. If the new tool has its own price book, you will map yours onto it in step 3.

Put all of it in one folder on your own computer, not inside either software. That folder is your safety net for the whole move.

Step 2: Audit what you actually use

Before rebuilding anything, open your old tool and write down what you genuinely use on a normal job: usually two or three templates, a handful of standard comments, and a price list. Most inspectors discover they use about a third of what they built over the years. The rest is clutter that does not need to migrate.

This audit also tells you what to test in the new tool. If you never use the scheduling add-on, its absence is not a dealbreaker. If you use the same roof template on every job, that template is the first thing to rebuild — and the first thing to test.

Step 3: Rebuild your templates in the new tool — then test with a real job

Set up the new account and rebuild only the templates from your audit. Do not try to recreate every comment you ever wrote; start with the ones that appear in most reports.

Then run a test: take a real past job — photos and notes from a report you already delivered — and build it end to end in the new tool. Photo to finding, finding to severity, estimate to PDF. Compare the finished PDF with the one you actually sent. If it reads the same and looks professional, your templates work. If something is off, fix it now, when no client is waiting.

This is also the moment to check the new tool's real cost against your old one. Step 4: Run both tools in parallel for one to two weeks

Do not cancel the old subscription yet. For the next one or two weeks, do your real jobs in the new tool and keep the old one open as a fallback. If a template is missing something on a live job, you can still finish the report the old way.

Two weeks is enough for most inspectors: that is usually four to eight real reports, which is a fair test of every template and every workflow you actually use. If the new tool survives two weeks of real jobs, it will survive the rest of the year.

Step 5: Move the jobs that are already booked

Check your calendar. Any inspection already scheduled should have its client details and property address in the new tool before the day arrives. Agents who email you expect the report link to arrive the same way it always has — they do not need to know you changed software, and from their side nothing should look different.

Step 6: Cancel the old plan — after the first real report ships

Cancel only after you have delivered at least one real, paid report from the new tool and the client received it without a problem. Check the old plan's renewal date first: cancelling a day after renewal means paying for another month you will not use. If the renewal is days away and you are mid-test, it is cheaper to let one more month run than to rush the switch.

Before the old account closes, confirm one last time that your exported PDFs and contacts are on your own computer. Then cancel, and keep the export folder. Some inspectors need a report from three years ago for a legal question; the folder is what answers it.

The short version

  • Export first: templates, past reports as PDFs, contacts, price book — into a folder you own.
  • Audit: list only what you actually use, and rebuild that — not the clutter.
  • Test with a real job: rebuild one past report end to end and compare the PDF.
  • Overlap: run both tools for one to two weeks of real jobs.
  • Move booked jobs: client details go into the new tool before inspection day.
  • Cancel last: after a real report ships cleanly, and before the next renewal.
